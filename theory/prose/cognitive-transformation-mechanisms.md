# Cognitive transformation mechanisms

The foundational vocabulary of the recursive distributed-intelligence theory: the local building blocks from which intelligent behaviour can emerge across scales, with `intelligence` reserved for the system level [[intelligence_when_organised_and_directed]].

## Why this vocabulary exists

If every component that contributes to cognition were already called intelligent, the theory would be circular: intelligence would emerge from smaller intelligences [[mechanism_when_it_serves_in_some_system]]. Instead, intelligence can emerge from the organised interaction of local transformation mechanisms that do not themselves need to possess the same kind of intelligence as the larger system [[whole_when_it_has_a_mechanism]].

## Cognitive transformation

A transformation is cognitive when the working of a directed system that the transformer belongs to takes its output up: a component of that system, possibly the same component at a later time, takes the output up and transforms it, or an evaluator judging under the system's criterion takes it up, transforms it, and its response returns into the system [[cognitive_when_taken_up_by_the_working_of_its_system]] [[working_takes_up_through_a_component]] [[working_takes_up_through_a_returning_evaluator]]. Being cognitive is therefore a role in a system, not a property of the transformation itself and not a relation to a lone taker: a transformation whose output nothing takes up is not cognitive, however much information it processes, and the same transformation becomes cognitive once the working of a directed system drawn round the transformer takes it up [[cognitive_when_taken_up_by_the_working_of_its_system]]. Whoever describes the phenomenon draws that boundary, and the same entity can lie inside several [[cognitive_when_taken_up_by_the_working_of_its_system]]. A taker that only records the output does not make it cognitive; the taker must transform it [[working_takes_up_through_a_component]]. An evaluator that takes the output up and judges it but never replies does not make it cognitive either: the revision a silent editor reads is no cognitive transformation, while the same revision is one once the editor's notes return to the team [[working_takes_up_through_a_returning_evaluator]]. A taker that nothing directs does not make it cognitive either: the sun's turning water into vapour is taken up by the cloud and turned into rain, and the sky is under no criterion, so it is not a cognitive transformation [[cognitive_when_taken_up_by_the_working_of_its_system]]. Whether the transformation compresses, expands, criticises, retrieves, translates, predicts, or reorganises its input does not matter [[cognitive_when_taken_up_by_the_working_of_its_system]]. Nor need it succeed: a wrong proposal that a critic takes up and rejects is still a cognitive transformation [[cognitive_when_taken_up_by_the_working_of_its_system]]. The taker may take the output up at any later time, and the boundary may span the interval: a paper read a century after it was written, by a reader working under a criterion of truth within a scholarship drawn across the century, was a cognitive transformation, and one that only an archive ever holds was not [[cognitive_when_taken_up_by_the_working_of_its_system]]. The taker may be the world that an action changes, when the boundary of the directed system is drawn to include it: a player's move that the board takes up, inside a game the player plays to win, is a cognitive transformation [[cognitive_when_taken_up_by_the_working_of_its_system]]. The taker may be the same component at a later time, because the self is divided along time: an author who re-reads and reworks their own draft for clarity performs a cognitive transformation, and the solitary writing is a recurrent cognitive system [[cognitive_when_taken_up_by_the_working_of_its_system]] [[recurrent_cognitive_when_directed]].

## Cognitive transformation mechanism

A cognitive transformation mechanism is a component whose output the working of its directed system takes up; this is the boundary-relative role, and a taker inside the boundary that does nothing with the output does not confer it, nor does a system that nothing directs [[mechanism_when_the_working_takes_up_its_output]]. A mechanism is always a mechanism in some system, and there is no other sense of the word: a translator whose translation a client takes up and answers is a mechanism in the exchange drawn round the two of them as it works towards a deal, and needs no system of their own beyond that [[mechanism_when_it_serves_in_some_system]]. Depending on the scale of analysis, a mechanism may be a specialised human cognitive process, a person, an LLM, a retrieval system, a critic or verifier, a memory system that transforms usable state, a software tool, or a team performing one transformation inside a larger workflow [[mechanism_when_it_serves_in_some_system]]. It may also be the environment: when the board's position is taken up and transformed by the player, inside a game drawn to include the board, the board is a mechanism, and a mechanism in the game [[mechanism_when_it_serves_in_some_system]] [[mechanism_when_the_working_takes_up_its_output]].

## States and representations are coupling surfaces

A passive repository, communication channel, or resource is not a cognitive transformation mechanism merely because a cognitive system uses it [[shared_state_when_stored_and_consumed]]. A state that one component passively holds and another component takes up is a coupling surface within the system: the surface through which local transformations become reusable [[shared_state_when_stored_and_consumed]]. Language, diagrams, symbols, embeddings, documents, code, APIs, task state, shared memory, physical signals, and institutional procedures can all serve as coupling surfaces [[shared_state_when_stored_and_consumed]]. A work that an archive holds and a later viewer takes up is a coupling surface across time, not a mechanism [[shared_state_when_stored_and_consumed]].

## Organised cognitive system

A system is an organised cognitive system when at least one of its components serves as a cognitive transformation mechanism within it, that is, when the components' transformations are taken up by one another inside a working that a criterion directs [[whole_when_it_has_a_mechanism]]. Organisation alone does not make one: a system whose components' transformations chain along uptake transforms as a system, but if nothing directs it, no component serves as a mechanism in it and it is not cognitive, whether it is a feed-forward pipeline that decodes a file and hashes the pixels, two colleagues who critique and revise under no stated criterion, or the weather [[mechanism_when_the_working_takes_up_its_output]] [[whole_when_it_has_a_mechanism]] [[transformation_when_components_chain]]. Many identical processors that never take up one another's output do not form one either [[whole_when_it_has_a_mechanism]]. No recurrence is required [[whole_when_it_has_a_mechanism]].

## Direction

A criterion directs a system only when it enters the working; a criterion that is merely attributed to a system directs nothing [[directed_when_component_takes_up_criterion]]. It enters from inside when a component of the system takes the criterion up, as a comparator takes up the integrity criterion or a reviewer takes up the brief [[directed_when_component_takes_up_criterion]]. It enters from outside when an evaluator judging under the criterion takes the system's output up and its response returns into the system, as an editor's notes return to the author [[directed_when_evaluator_returns]]. Direction can thus be supplied by the task, the environment, or an evaluator [[directed_when_evaluator_returns]].

## Intelligence

Within this theory, intelligence is the working of an organised cognitive system under the criterion that directs it, not a quantity the system owns [[intelligence_when_organised_and_directed]]. A system that no criterion directs does not realise intelligence, whether no criterion is attributed to it or one merely is; neither does a collection of uncoupled components that has been given one [[intelligence_when_organised_and_directed]]. A feed-forward pipeline with a comparator that takes the integrity criterion up realises intelligence under that criterion; the same pipeline with the criterion only attributed to it does not [[intelligence_when_organised_and_directed]]. Intelligence is judged at a boundary and does not descend: a pipeline that serves as a mechanism in a backup system working towards safe storage does not itself realise intelligence under that criterion [[intelligence_when_organised_and_directed]] [[mechanism_when_the_working_takes_up_its_output]].

## Recurrence

A system is recurrent when the component that takes up another component's output and transforms it feeds its own output back into that component [[recurrent_when_output_returns]]. Recurrence is structural: a generator and an adder that feed each other random numbers are recurrent [[recurrent_when_output_returns]]. A recurrent cognitive system is a recurrent system that is also an organised cognitive system, so that the re-entry happens inside a working that a criterion directs [[recurrent_cognitive_when_directed]]. A recurrent cognitive system can revise: a mechanism's later transformation acts on the response to its earlier output, so no state at that mechanism is final [[revision_when_recurrent]]. For re-entry that nothing directs, revision is not derived: the generator and adder are not derived to revise, while a studio whose planner takes up buildability is a recurrent cognitive system and is [[revision_when_recurrent]] [[recurrent_cognitive_when_directed]].

## Scale relativity and recursive nesting

Recurrence and recursive nesting name different features [[nested_when_whole_is_mechanism]]. Recurrence is temporal re-entry: outputs feed back into later transformation [[recurrent_when_output_returns]]. Recursive nesting is scale-relative composition: an organised cognitive system at one boundary that serves as a cognitive transformation mechanism at a larger boundary is nested in that larger system [[nested_when_whole_is_mechanism]]. The same entity can therefore be modelled as a cognitive system at one boundary and as a mechanism at a larger one, e.g. neural processes in a person, people in a team, a human with an AI and tools in a composite, teams and records in an organisation [[nested_when_whole_is_mechanism]]. Both boundaries must be directed: a person whose recall takes up an orientation criterion is a cognitive system at her own boundary, and a mechanism in a team whose reviewer takes up the review criterion, so she is nested in the team [[nested_when_whole_is_mechanism]]. A feed-forward pipeline that nothing directs at its own scale serves as a mechanism in the backup system that takes up its digest, but it is not nested there, because it is not a cognitive system at its own boundary [[nested_when_whole_is_mechanism]] [[mechanism_when_the_working_takes_up_its_output]]. What the larger boundary sees the system transform is not stated separately: a system transforms what a chain of its components transforms when each takes up the previous output, and nothing that one component transforms alone, so the system's transformation is its integration [[transformation_when_components_chain]]. The chain extends as long as a further component takes up what the chain has produced [[transformation_composes_along_uptake]].

## A person is not the privileged minimum unit

The analytical question is at what boundary treating a collection of transformations as one cognitive system best explains the behaviour of interest [[whole_when_it_has_a_mechanism]].
